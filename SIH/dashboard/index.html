PHONE-TO-VEHICLE ROLL/PITCH ALIGNMENT

Gravity-Based Leveling (Phase 3)

ALIGNMENT METHOD

Gravity-based leveling via Rodrigues minimal rotation

The current Phase 3 implementation performs ONLY phone-to-vehicle roll and pitch alignment. By averaging the static acceleration vector, the smartphone's coordinate frame is rotated such that its Z-axis aligns with true gravity.

CURRENT LIMITATION

Absolute Yaw is NOT Solved

  • Why gravity isn't enough: Gravity only provides a 2D vertical reference (pitch and roll). Yaw exists in the orthogonal horizontal plane and cannot be observed from a static gravity vector alone.
  • Why other sensors fail here: Magnetometers suffer from severe hard-iron/soft-iron distortions inside a metal vehicle chassis. Standard GNSS or motion-based yaw estimation can be highly unreliable due to urban canyons and vehicle dynamics.

OBSERVATION & QUANTITATIVE FINDING

Coordinate alignment alone does not eliminate long-term inertial drift.

Phase 3 successfully levels the smartphone's gravity vector to machine precision (det R = 1.000000). However, because consumer MEMS accelerometers carry residual in-run biases (~0.05 m/s²), rotating the sensor frame without a velocity constraint (NHC or AI Speed) still leads to quadratic divergence.
